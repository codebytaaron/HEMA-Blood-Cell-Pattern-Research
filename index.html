How it works

  1. 1
    Upload

    Choose a stained blood smear image with one white blood cell near the centre.

  2. 2
    Compare

    A MobileNetV2 model in your browser compares it with patterns learned from 3,827 cell images labelled by clinical pathologists [1].

  3. 3
    Read

    You get a score for each cell type. On 957 held-out images from the same dataset, the top score matched the pathologists' label 97.8% of the time. Expect lower accuracy on images from other labs or stains.

The four cell types

Private by design

Your image is analyzed in your browser and is never uploaded or stored. The page loads its code and fonts from public CDNs (jsDelivr and Google Fonts). HEMA has no accounts, cookies or analytics.

Research only

HEMA is an educational tool, not a medical device. It learned only from normal cells of people without infection, blood disease or cancer [1], so it cannot detect leukemia or any other condition. Its scores are not the chance that anyone is ill.
